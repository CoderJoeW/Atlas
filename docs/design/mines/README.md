# Mines

Seven mining derricks — Coal, Iron, Redstone, Gold, Emerald, Diamond and Netherite — built from the
reactive-states chart in [`references/mining-system-reactive-states.jpg`](references/mining-system-reactive-states.jpg).
That chart is the authority for how the system looks.

## What a mine does

A mine turns stored power straight into ore. It does not touch the world around it: there is no
deposit to find, nothing to deplete, and no terrain is broken. Build one anywhere a cable reaches
and it digs forever.

Each tick a mine pulls from its neighbours, and when it holds enough power it spends the cost and
drops one ore at `+0.5, +1.5, +0.5` — one block above the deck, so the haul falls clear of the rig
and onto whatever the player has run underneath it.

The tiers differ only in what they dig, what a haul costs and how long the bore takes:

| Mine | Output | Storage | Power per haul | Bore |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Coal | `COAL` | 10 | 2 | 40t (2s) |
| Iron | `RAW_IRON` | 20 | 5 | 60t (3s) |
| Redstone | `REDSTONE` | 20 | 5 | 60t (3s) |
| Gold | `RAW_GOLD` | 30 | 8 | 80t (4s) |
| Emerald | `EMERALD` | 50 | 14 | 120t (6s) |
| Diamond | `DIAMOND` | 60 | 18 | 160t (8s) |
| Netherite | `ANCIENT_DEBRIS` | 100 | 30 | 200t (10s) |

All seven share `Mine`, an abstract `PowerBlock` in `utility/block`. A subclass supplies only its
block id, cycle length, haul cost and output material — there is no per-mine behaviour.

## Two states, one block definition

The chart draws two states per mine: **idle / no power** and **digging**. These are one CraftEngine
block definition with a `powered` boolean property and two appearances, flipped by
`updatePoweredState()` — the same shape the factories and the Auto Smelter use. `getVisualStateBlockId()`
always returns the base id; the appearance is the property, not a second block.

Both appearances are forced `state: barrier` with an `entity_renderer`, so neither claims a slot
from the exhausted auto-state pools.

## The shaft-mouth model

`models/block/custom/mine_shaft.json` is shared by all appearances and is generated by
[`build_shaft.py`](build_shaft.py).

The mine is a **timbered shaft mouth sunk into a reinforced pad** — a raised brow over the opening,
heavy portal timbering standing proud of the mass, rails running out of the dark, and spoil heaped
beside the entrance. It fits inside its own cell: there is no tower and nothing overhangs.

**Four tower silhouettes were rejected before this one**, and the reasons are worth keeping,
because they are all the same mistake:

1. *A four-legged lattice mast with a box on top* — the retired Iron Mine's exact trap. A straight
   lattice tower reads as a **watchtower or pylon**, never a mine.
2. *A fat screw with five broad flights* — the flights were 12 wide and 1.5 thick, so it read as
   **a stack of plates on a crate**.
3. *A bit hung inside a mast with a crown* — the cage won and **the bit hid behind its own
   supporting structure**.
4. *A long twisting auger cone* — the most legible of the four as a drill, and still rejected.

The lesson is that **the tall-machine-on-a-pad premise was itself wrong**. A low shaft mouth reads
as "mine" instantly, needs no tower, and does not loom over a base.

**The tunnel is real geometry, not a painted-on hole.** The pad is built as a west wall, an east
wall and a brow, with a void between them and a shaft face at the back. That face takes the **deck**
texture, whose centre is already a dark bore ringed by a steel collar — and whose digging variants
already glow out of that bore. Look into the mouth of a working mine and you see the glow down the
hole, with no new art.

**Three sides are solid across y 4–12, which is where the power cable's hub and arms sit.** The
fourth side is the mouth, open on purpose — you do not wire into the opening. `MineTest` pins the
three solid faces so a future shape change cannot quietly break the wiring.

Because the pad is hollowed by the tunnel it is not a full cube, so the block sets
`can-occlude: false` and `is-view-blocking: false`; otherwise neighbours cull their faces against it
and the world shows through. Nothing leaves the cell any more, so the model needs no
`entity_culling` override.

## Facing

A mouth has a front, so mines are `DIRECTIONAL_OPPOSITE`: the opening turns back toward whoever
placed it. The `facing` property is `horizontal_direction`, which matters — `getPlayerFacing`
answers `UP` for any block set on the ground, that value fails to apply, and the listener falls back
to the player's own facing. `Mine` also clamps any non-horizontal face to north, so a block restored
from disk with no stored facing cannot end up pointing at the sky.

The model is authored with the mouth opening toward **+Z (south)**, matching the conveyor belt, so
the four facings are one model at yaw 0 / 180 / -90 / +90 — eight appearances per mine, four
facings times two states.

**Each facing needs its own ore position.** `position` on a renderer element is an absolute offset
from the block corner, and `yaw` turns the display entity in place *without* orbiting it. Reusing a
single position would strand the ore inside the pad on three of the four rotations. `MineTest`
checks that every facing puts the ore out at its own mouth.

## Art

Idle art is **shared across all seven mines**: `mine_deck`, `mine_side`, `mine_tower`, `mine_bottom`.
The chart draws every idle rig in the same charcoal, and only the digging state carries colour.

Digging art is per mine — `{id}_deck_digging` and `{id}_side_digging` — where the bore shaft and the
vent row ignite in that ore's colour:

| Mine | Glow |
|:--|:--|
| Coal | ember orange-red `255,107,44` |
| Iron | molten orange `255,154,46` |
| Redstone | electric red `255,32,32` |
| Gold | gold `255,215,0` |
| Emerald | green `44,255,140` |
| Diamond | pale cyan `77,232,255` |
| Netherite | magenta-violet `224,64,251` |

Note that the mining system's energy colour is **per ore**, not one colour for the whole system —
unlike power (amber) and transport (green). The chart is explicit about this.

All eighteen textures were generated with the Artlist MCP (Nano Banana Pro) at 2K and downscaled to
512×512, deriving every face and state by image-to-image from the first deck so the set stays
consistent.

## Telling the mines apart

Idle art being shared means a placed Iron Mine and a placed Gold Mine are the same object. What
distinguishes them is the **ore shown at the shaft mouth**: `entity_renderer` takes a *list* of
elements, so every mine renders the pad plus the vanilla item it produces — `minecraft:raw_iron` on
the Iron Mine, `minecraft:ancient_debris` on the Netherite Mine — sitting on the rails in the
opening, scaled to 0.9 and billboarded to face the player.

The mouth is the right place for it: the ore is silhouetted against the dark tunnel, and against
the glow once the mine is digging. An earlier pass put it at 0.6 scale in the middle of the machine
and it was simply lost behind the geometry. `MineTest` pins the scale and, per facing, the
position.

That costs no textures and states the mine's output in the most direct way available: the ore
itself. `MineTest` asserts the mounted item matches the block class's `output` material, since
nothing at runtime ties the config to the Kotlin.

## No GUI icons

Unlike the solar panel, the mines have no painted inventory icon — the derrick model renders in the
slot as it does for the Crusher, Auto Smelter and factories. A two-block model overflows a 16px
slot, so if the in-hand look reads badly, seven `item/custom/{id}` icons wired through a
`minecraft:select` on `display_context` are the fix.
